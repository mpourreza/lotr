*Finder of the Ring in the river* · Third Age

## Overview & Essence

Déagol is a Stoor Hobbit who fishes the Gladden Fields with his cousin Sméagol. In T.A. 2463 he finds a golden ring in the water. Sméagol demands it as a birthday present, strangles him, and hides the murder. The Ring passes to Gollum and so begins the long road to Bilbo and Frodo.

## Key Characteristics & Attributes

- **Type/Category:** Character (Stoor Hobbit)
- **Origins/Creation:** Lived among the Stoors near the Gladden; cousin and friend of Sméagol.
- **Key Affiliations/Associations:** Sméagol/Gollum; the One Ring; the river Anduin.
- **Primary Role/Purpose:** To be the Ring's first finder after Isildur's loss—unwittingly.

## Detailed History & Narrative Arc

- **The find.** A fish pulls him under; his hand closes on gold.
- **The murder.** Sméagol kills him on his birthday and takes the Precious.
- **After.** His body is hidden; the Stoors do not know how the Ring entered their kind.

## Notable Connections & Relationships

- **[Gollum]({{ '/characters/gollum/' | relative_url }}):** Cousin who murdered him for the Ring.
- **[The One Ring]({{ '/people/one-ring/' | relative_url }}):** Object of the killing.
- **[The Anduin]({{ '/locations/anduin/' | relative_url }}):** River where it lay after Isildur.

## Legacy & Significance

Déagol appears only in Gandalf's reconstruction of the Ring's history. His death is the first crime the Ring inspires in the Third Age.
